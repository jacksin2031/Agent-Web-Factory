# Market and portfolio research for auto-selected sites

Use this reference when the user asks the factory to choose website ideas, niches, positioning, or an entire batch portfolio instead of supplying exact site concepts.

## Use current evidence

Do not choose a commercial niche only from model memory. Use current web/search/provider information available at execution time and record the evidence date.

For each proposed site, investigate enough to answer:

- Who is the primary user and what concrete job are they trying to do?
- What existing products/pages already solve it?
- What is missing, cumbersome, expensive, outdated, localized poorly, or not AI-assisted in a useful way?
- Is the problem repeatable enough to justify a standalone site?
- What acquisition channel is plausible: search, community, direct utility, integrations, referrals, social, or paid acquisition?
- What monetization model is plausible: ads, subscription, usage-based, affiliate, lead generation, paid download, sponsorship, or no monetization?
- Does the proposed AI capability improve the task rather than merely generate generic text?

## Evidence quality

Prefer direct/current evidence over speculative keyword claims:

- live search results and competing products;
- official product/pricing pages;
- current platform/app marketplaces;
- current trend/community discussions when relevant;
- public search-trend data when available;
- reputable market/research sources for broad demand claims;
- current advertising/provider eligibility rules when monetization depends on them.

Do not invent keyword volume, CPC, revenue, conversion rate, traffic, market size, competitor usage, or search ranking. Label estimates as estimates and record their source/method.

## Portfolio selection

Before building a batch, produce an internal portfolio plan and reject/merge concepts that have the same core job-to-be-done.

Prefer a portfolio in which sites differ on at least two meaningful dimensions such as:

- user/job;
- interaction/tool behavior;
- data source or workflow;
- information architecture;
- monetization logic;
- acquisition channel;
- defensible localization/domain expertise.

Changing a city, keyword, color, persona label, or AI system prompt is not sufficient differentiation by itself.

## Search and content quality

Do not mass-produce doorway pages, scraped/copied pages, spun location pages, auto-generated articles with no user value, fake freshness, or pages whose only purpose is to rank and redirect users elsewhere.

When advertising is planned, prioritize original useful functionality/content and navigable product pages rather than building only for ad inventory.

## Higher-risk topics

Treat health, legal, financial, safety, employment eligibility, immigration, and other consequential topics as higher-risk. Prefer authoritative sources, deterministic rules/calculators where possible, prominent limitations, and current jurisdiction-specific references. Do not position generic AI output as professional advice.

## Research output

For auto-selected batches, store a concise research artifact under `.web-factory/reports/portfolio-research.json` containing, per concept:

- evidence date;
- target user/job;
- current alternatives/competitors reviewed;
- observed gap;
- planned unique capability;
- AI role, if any;
- monetization hypothesis;
- principal risk/unknown;
- decision: build / merge / reject.

The artifact should contain summarized evidence and source references, not copied articles or unnecessary personal data.
